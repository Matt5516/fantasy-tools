Dynasty Draft Pick Trade Values: What a 1st Buys in Week 7

What a 2027 early, mid and late 1st, a 2028 1st and 2nds buy in dynasty trades, 1QB and superflex, plus 11 pick-for-player deals that grade fair.

As of Oct 21, 2026, a 2027 early 1st is worth 4,127 in 1QB (4,581 in superflex), about the same as Tee Higgins. A mid 1st is 1,864 (45% of an early), a late 1st 871 and a 2028 1st 1,508. An early 2nd is only 429.

By DynastyMath Staff · Published October 21, 2026 · Numbers as of October 21, 2026 · 5 min read

Everybody wants to know what a pick is worth. Fewer people know what it buys. Here are the prices, the players who sit next to each price, and the pick-for-player deals that land inside the calculator's fair range. Values as of Oct 21, 2026.

Values use a 0 to 10,000 scale. Players are priced on it the same way, so a pick and a player are directly comparable. Superflex has its own set of values, and every pick is higher there.

The slot matters more than the year. An early 2027 1st is 4,127. A mid 1st is 1,864. A late 1st is 871. The early pick is worth 2.2 times the mid and 4.7 times the late. If a league-mate says "a first is a first," ask which one.

Where they sit among players. In 1QB, the early 1st would rank 36 by value, ahead of all but 35 players. The mid 1st would rank 83. The late 1st lands at 120. That is a long way down for a pick that sounds like a first-round prize.

A pick with no slot. Some leagues just list "2027 1st". It is priced at 1,885 in 1QB, which is a mid pick. Treat an unspecified 1st as a mid and you will not be far off.

The 2028 1st. At 1,508 it is worth 81% of a 2027 mid 1st and 1.7 times a late 1st. It also sits above a 2027 late 1st.

Second-rounders are small. An early 2nd is 429. A mid 1st is 4.3 times that. A 2028 2nd is 181 and a late 2nd is 121. Think of a 2nd as a throw-in, not a trade chip.

Superflex pays more for everything

Every pick in the table is higher in superflex. The smallest bump among the 1sts is the early 2027 1st at +11%. The biggest is the late 1st at +19%. The largest in the table is the late 2nd, +33%. In superflex the early 1st (4,581) would rank 37. The mid 1st (2,126) ranks 74. Compare your league's format before you quote a price from memory.

Early 1st. In 1QB, Luther Burden is a clean match. In superflex, Garrett Wilson is. The ask is the same: a starter for the pick, not a prospect for the pick.

Mid 1st. C.J. Stroud is the lone fair 1-for-1 target in 1QB, age 25. In superflex, Tucker Kraft fits. Late 1st. Marvin Harrison is the 1QB match and Makai Lemon the superflex match. A late 1st buys depth, not a core piece.

2028 1st. KC Concepcion in 1QB and Cam Ward in superflex. If you hold a 2028 1st, it buys a player now.

Two picks for one. Brenton Strange costs a late 1st plus an early 2nd in 1QB. Bucky Irving costs a mid 1st plus an early 2nd in superflex.

How to use this

  1. Name the slot. Early, mid and late are three different prices.
  2. Use your format. The superflex number is the one to quote in a superflex league.
  3. Don't pay for two picks like they add up. The second asset is discounted.
  4. Sell a pick when your starter is thin. The fair list above is the target shelf.
  5. Price your own. Run your deal through the dynasty trade analyzer and see the pick board on the rookie pick values page.

Frequently asked questions

What is a 2027 1st round pick worth in dynasty?

As of Oct 21, 2026, a 2027 early 1st is 4,127 in 1QB and 4,581 in superflex. A mid 1st is 1,864 and 2,126. A late 1st is 871 and 1,037.

Is a 2028 1st worth less than a 2027 1st?

Less than an early or mid 2027 1st, but more than a late one. A 2028 1st is 1,508 in 1QB against 871 for a 2027 late 1st and 1,864 for a mid.

What player is worth an early 1st?

In 1QB, Luther Burden (3,993) is within 5% of the pick's 4,127. In superflex, Garrett Wilson (4,734) is within 5% of 4,581. Jeremiyah Love sits near the same price in 1QB (4,152) but is listed Questionable.

Can a 2nd round pick buy a starter?

Rarely. An early 2nd is 429 in 1QB and 531 in superflex. It matches depth pieces such as Jake Ferguson (423), not core starters.

Where do these pick prices come from?

They come from the same expert and draft-market values that price players on the site. Sleeper dynasty drafts supplied market data today; Fantasy Football Calculator had no dynasty data that day.
